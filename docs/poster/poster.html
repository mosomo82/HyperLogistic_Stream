Problem Statement

  • Unexpected transit delays bottleneck dock doors and downstream hubs.
  • Static schedules cannot react to weather, airport ground stops or a backlog building up.
  • Goal: predict arrival-delay change (Δ = ArrDelay − DepDelay) per trip in real time, and show how a hub compares with its typical day.
  • Flights serve as a public proxy for hub-to-hub freight movement.

Dataset Description

BTS On-Time Performance2018–2024 flights, 24 US hubs, ≥11 GB. Train 8.0M, validate 1.67M (2024).
NOAA ISD WeatherHourly wind, visibility, ceiling, temperature at 40 stations.
FAA NAS StatusLive airport delays and closures.
NWS WeatherLive METAR observations per hub.
OpenSky NetworkLive aircraft positions for the radar map.
Baseline and features
Typical-delay table: 24 hubs × local hour × weekday (3,878 cells, training years only) powers “today vs. typical”.

14 model features: departure delay, distance, dep./arr. hour (UTC), weekday, lane mean delay, plus 4 destination and 4 origin weather fields.

Honest split: trained on 2018–2023 except 2020, evaluated on 2024, which the model never saw.

Results & Evaluation

Weather adds about 1%. MAE on 2024: GBT 10.53, no change 12.75, lane mean 10.80.

Wins on early flights, loses to “no change” on on-time and late ones. Not a severe-delay alarm.

4,448 records/s best (orange); 4 cores (dark) beat 2 by ~22%. Provisional: 4 batches per config.

Conclusion & Future Work

  • Kafka → Spark → Medallion → dashboard, with repeat-safe writes and 23 automated tests in CI. Big delays are still missed.
  • Next: severe-delay classifier, full benchmark re-run, real freight data.
